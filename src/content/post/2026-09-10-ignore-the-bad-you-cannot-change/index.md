Want to stress more, or don't feel helpless enough? Try to move your attention to all the negative events outside your area of influence.

## The 7 Habits

Stephen Covey published _The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People_ in 1989. It more or less says:

1. **Be proactive.** You are a product of your choices, not your circumstances. Choice to improve what's within your control.
2. **Begin with the end in mind.** Decide what goal you wish to accomplish, before you land on which task to do.
3. **Put first things first.** Important beats urgent.
4. **Think win-win.** Agreements that leave someone bitter do not last. Winning _over_ others isn't winning.
5. **Seek first to understand, then to be understood.** Listen before you argue, or they will not hear you anyway.
6. **Synergize.** The combination is better than the parts.
7. **Sharpen the saw.** Rest, and get better at the instrument.

The first three are a private victory: get your own house in order. The next three are a public one: work with other people without turning it into a fight. The last is maintenance.

## Control, influence, concern

Within the first habit, a useful tool I find is the 3 circles, and where..

**Circle of control** is the stuff you decide. How you spend the morning. Whether you go for a run. How you talk in a 1:1. You change these by acting.

**Circle of influence** is the stuff you cannot decide, but you can lean on. A project's pace. A client's call. Your health, your career. You don't own the outcome. You still have a hand in it.

**Circle of concern** is everything that gets under your skin and will not move because you stared at it. World politics. Natural disasters. What a colleague thinks of you. Who sits in the White House.

[Sabine Hahn's write-up](https://www.sabine-hahn.com/en/post/stephen-covey-s-circle-of-influence) is a clean version of this.

The point of the model is not the labels. It's where your Tuesday went. How much of the day did you spend annoyed at something you cannot touch? How much did you contribute to what you could?

_Covey: proactive people work the inner rings, and those rings grow. Reactive people sit in the outer ring, complain, and watch the inner ones shrink._

## Ignore the bad you cannot change

The advice is usually drawn as three clean rings, but life is a grayscale. Your influence on a team is not the same as your influence on a company or community. Focus more on things the closer they are to you.

- Control what you can control.
- Improve what you can improve.
- Find joy in what's outside your influence.
- Ignore what feels bad outside your influence.

Rain is outside my influence. I can enjoy the sound of it, or I can put on a jacket. Complaining about the weather is the third option, and it's one that can feed a feeling of helplessness if done too much.

> I am not a product of my circumstances. I am a product of my decisions.

It's not necessarily true. But it's an extremely useful little lie that brings out the best of us.
